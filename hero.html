Three engines,
one corpus, one textbook.

Three analysers of functional harmony — musWM, AnalysisGNN and AugmentedNet — read the same 335 scores of the When in Rome corpus (Gotham et al., 2023). Their Roman numerals are aligned by bar and beat, and two kinds of evidence are kept apart. A textbook grade asks whether each label is written as Kostka, Payne & Almén’s Tonal Harmony writes the chord musWM read; because that reference comes from musWM’s reading, it is not a contest between the engines.

What the figures support. musWM’s labels are transparent and follow the textbook’s naming rules (99.6%, a consistency check). In unambiguous bars its root and bass match the score at 99.3% and 100%. That makes it an interpretable reference to compare against AnalysisGNN and AugmentedNet, not a validated arbiter.

The textbook grades measure tolerant agreement with a label derived from musWM’s own reading of the chord; they are not an accuracy against an independent reference.

The reference label is built from musWM’s key, root, bass and notes, and the other two engines are judged in musWM’s key, so musWM’s score is high largely by construction. Read these bars as “how often a label is written as the book writes musWM’s reading”, not as accuracy. A triad and a seventh on the same root and bass count as the same label.

Status of these results

Everything reported here is a first set of results, published so that the comparison, the textbook criterion and the figures can be inspected and questioned. On 15 September 2026 an alignment fault was found: musWM’s bar numbers had been counted on the MIDI timeline, so in some works its labels sat on the wrong written bar. The corpus was rebuilt with every musWM label placed on its written bar, 332 of the 335 works now pass an automatic check that each engine’s reported notes exist in the bar it is shown on (the other three are listed for review), and the textbook rule set was checked against the book. All figures on this site come from that rebuild; the changes are listed under Corrections.
