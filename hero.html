Three engines,
one corpus, one textbook.

Three analysers of functional harmony — musWM, AnalysisGNN and AugmentedNet — read the same 335 scores of the When in Rome corpus (Gotham et al., 2023). Their Roman numerals are aligned by bar and beat and held against a single yardstick: the label Kostka, Payne & Almén’s Tonal Harmony assigns to the chord in question. Each verdict is a 1 or a 0, fixed once and not revisited.

Gotham, M., Micchi, G., Nápoles López, N., & Sailor, M. (2023). When in Rome: A meta-corpus of functional harmony. Transactions of the International Society for Music Information Retrieval, 6(1), 150–166. doi:10.5334/tismir.165

Status of these results

Everything reported here is a first set of results. The comparison, the textbook criterion and the figures are published so that they can be inspected and questioned while detailed validation is under way. When that validation is complete, the final accuracy figures may differ from those shown by up to ±5 percentage points for AnalysisGNN and AugmentedNet and by about ±1 point for musWM. This margin applies to accuracy under the present textbook criterion; comparison with independent expert annotation measures something different and may move all three engines further. How the margin was estimated is set out on Ratings.
